ABOUT JEJU

Jeju is a unique destination with beautiful landscapes that formed from the eruption of a volcano approximately two million years ago. The island is oval-shaped and about 80km from the southern tip of the Korean Peninsula. Jeju has several UNESCO natural World Heritage sites. For geographical and historical reasons, Jeju is famous for three things: rocks, women and wind.

WEATHER

Jeju has a humid subtropical climate with four distinct seasons. The island is warmer than the other parts of Korea because it is surrounded by sea. Spring is from March to May and Jeju is filled with canola and cherry blossom flowers. Summer is from June to August, with frequent heavy rain. Autumn is from September to November, desiduous trees colour the island yellow and red. Winter is from December to February, it snow occasionally. October to February is the peak season for tangerine picking.

HAENYEO

Jeju has a community of women called Haenyeo. Haenyeo is a female seafood diver in Jeju. The oldest divers in the community are in their eighties. They dive down ten metres to catch seafood without oxygen masks. There is a Haenyeo museum and a school where you can experience a dive with the Haenyeo.
